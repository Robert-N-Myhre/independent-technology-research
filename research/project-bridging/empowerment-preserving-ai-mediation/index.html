    Empowerment-Preserving AI Mediation

    Status: Research concept — proposed empirical study; no participant data collected, no study conducted, no results claimed.
    Research program: Project Bridging · Independent Technology Research
    Relationship to prior work: A bounded empirical follow-up to AI as a Scaffold for Human Connection (Paper 1; conceptual working paper, not peer reviewed).

    Research question

    Under what conditions can AI-assisted interpersonal communication improve mutual understanding without distorting what people intend to say or undermining their informed control over what they communicate?

    AI can help people phrase messages more smoothly, but smooth communication is not necessarily accurate communication. Assistance may preserve meaning, clarify differences, or unintentionally remove disagreement and create a misleading impression of mutual understanding. The aim is not to reduce AI use: people may legitimately value ongoing assistance, translation, accessibility support, or independent conversations with AI.

    Proposed hypothesis

    In bounded human-to-human communication tasks, reflective AI scaffolding—which helps participants clarify their own intent, consider ambiguity, and ask questions without composing the final message for them—will yield more accurate partner-specific understanding and less unintended meaning distortion than AI-assisted rewriting. Unassisted communication will provide a baseline.

    This is a prediction, not a finding. Rewriting may perform equally well or better; reflection may introduce its own biases or demand additional effort; neither intervention may help.

    Candidate empirical comparison

    A randomized, consent-based study with adults would compare:

    1. Unassisted communication: People write to one another without AI mediation.
    2. AI-assisted rewriting: Participants can accept, edit, or reject AI-proposed wording.
    3. Reflective scaffolding: AI prompts clarification and offers possible interpretations; participants retain message composition.

    Before assistance, senders would record the central points and uncertainties they intend to express. Following a bounded exchange, recipients would independently describe what they understood. Evaluation would consider sender-confirmed intended meaning, recipient interpretation accuracy, recognition of genuine disagreement, perceived understanding, message provenance, participant control, and self-reported authenticity. Independent raters and predefined scoring rubrics would reduce circular reliance on the same AI that generated the messages. The design must distinguish legitimate changes of mind from unintended distortion.

    Feasibility and limitations

    A possible four-month investigation would begin with a controlled model-behavior evaluation using expert-reviewed scenarios, followed by a small randomized human-participant study only if appropriate ethics review, methodological expertise, recruitment, and funding are available. Sample size and statistical analysis require a prospective power/precision assessment. A short study cannot establish lasting relationship quality, long-term dependency, or general effects across cultures and accessibility needs.

    Scope within Project Bridging

    This study isolates the communication-support and participant-agency questions (primarily H3 and a bounded precursor to H7) in Project Bridging. It does not test compatibility prediction, longitudinal conversational profiles, AI-mediated introductions, or the proposed three-plane architecture. Favorable results would support one limited mechanism; null or adverse results could challenge it without settling the broader vision.

    Research posture: Independent, exploratory, falsifiable. Human agency means informed choice over assistance and expression—not mandatory independence from AI tools. This public concept is a proposed investigation, not an empirical publication.
